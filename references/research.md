# NFL research guide

## Schedule, rest and travel

1. Open the [NFL schedule](https://www.nfl.com/schedules/) and matching game page. Use official season, week and playoff labels. Cross-check kickoff and status with the club or [ESPN scoreboard](https://www.espn.com/nfl/scoreboard). Check flex changes, TBD fixtures, neutral sites and international games. Include the next season in the search if published and needed to locate the next meeting. NFL season labels carry into the following calendar year's playoffs.
2. For a local date, run `python3 scripts/context.py window --date YYYY-MM-DD` and check every returned Eastern date, then filter by the exact UTC interval. A week or playoff-round request includes every official game in that group. A single broad date response from a provider can be truncated. Check the official count.
3. After saving ESPN scoreboard JSON, `python3 scripts/context.py select week11.json --season 2026 --week 11` filters and sorts it. The endpoint pattern is `https://site.api.espn.com/apis/site/v2/sports/football/nfl/scoreboard?dates=2026&seasontype=2&week=11`. Pass multiple adjacent responses for Manila dates or next-meeting searches. Use `--date YYYY-MM-DD`, `--matchup IND HOU`, or `--round "wild card"` as appropriate. For playoffs use `seasontype=3` and the provider round number (1, 2, 3, or 5). Confirm the NFL round and venue. The helper filters the data provided, so check completeness before relying on an empty result.
4. Find the last game actually played by each team and its actual venue. Count the difference in kickoff dates in America/New_York using `python3 scripts/context.py rest PREVIOUS_ISO CURRENT_ISO` with offsets. Verify actual bye weeks from the schedule, flag short turnaround, and distinguish international or neutral sites from a team's home venue. For season openers identify the reference game and whether it was preseason. If no comparable prior game is known, give the available context without inventing a rest value.
5. Use sourced stadium coordinates and `python3 scripts/context.py miles --origin LAT LON --destination LAT LON`. Label great-circle distance as an **approximate venue-to-venue estimate**. Same stadium is zero venue-to-venue miles. Trips via the team base, flight routing, overnight timing, and arrival plans require separate sourcing. Rest and distance may describe overlapping effects.

## Availability, weather and roof

- Use the [NFL injury report](https://www.nfl.com/injuries/) and the latest official team report or update. Identify which practice days and designation have published, including estimated practices and late changes. Read practice participation (DNP, LP, FP) separately from game status (Out, Doubtful, Questionable or no designation). After release, read both official inactive lists and late roster moves. [NFL pregame procedures](https://operations.nfl.com/gameday/pre-game/countdown-to-kickoff) specify the standard inactive deadline 90 minutes before kickoff.
- Establish whether the venue is outdoor, fixed-roof, or retractable. For retractable venues, seek a dated team, venue or league roof decision. If pending, give open and closed scenarios when exposure matters. Check a kickoff-hour stadium point forecast from [NWS](https://www.weather.gov/) in the US or the local national meteorological service abroad. Report sustained wind and gusts, precipitation and temperature, and identify the forecast issuance or observation time. A daily forecast or weather at an airport alone is weaker evidence. For fixed covered venues, verify the venue and distinguish playing conditions from outdoor travel conditions.
- Describe the mechanism and market affected by a plausible scenario, such as replacement QB play, line shuffles, diminished kicking range, or passing conditions. A pending list before the deadline can be routine. Hold a market if the unresolved status could reverse it. When a decision arrives, verify the updated price and repeat that market's comparison.

## Scoring, efficiency and matchup

- Use the [NFL team statistics](https://www.nfl.com/stats/team-stats/), official gamebooks or box scores, and [nflverse team data](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/reference/load_team_stats.html). State season and sample dates, points scored and allowed, and the last four completed games or the available count. Keep preseason separate. Note opponent quality, turnovers, defensive or special-teams scoring, garbage time, overtime and personnel changes if they change the interpretation.
- For [nflverse play-by-play](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/reference/load_pbp.html), specify the provider's definition or your exact population. A reproducible default takes valid rush and pass plays with EPA, including sacks and scrambles, and excludes kneels, spikes, special teams and no-plays. Use mean EPA and the share with EPA above zero. Use the same population for season and recent splits. For defense report opponent EPA and success rate allowed. If sample coverage is partial, say where it ends. Never conflate season totals for passing and rushing EPA with per-play rates without compatible denominators.
- Use relevant verified splits such as early-down pass tendency, dropback versus rush efficiency, explosive-play rates, pace and neutral script, drive finishing, or pass protection versus rush pressure. Sack rate is a sack rate. For actual pressure, check [NFL Next Gen Stats](https://nextgenstats.nfl.com/) or another source with a documented definition. Use historical box scores, player usage and film reporting to explain the mechanism.
- Look for stale or blocked pages, cached data, timestamp conflicts and incomplete weeks. Try a direct alternate that covers the same fact. If evidence remains incomplete, say what was verified, use qualified context where relevant, and hold just the markets that depend on the missing evidence. For past kickoffs use only reports and information available before the game. Exclude final scores and postgame reporting about the target game from a historical pregame assessment.
