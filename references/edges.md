# NFL market comparison

Evaluate the full-game spread, total, and moneyline individually. Record each side, number, price, named sportsbook, source, and observation time with time zone. Verify the book, status, and game date as well as market period. Keep the user's supplied prices or requested book first. Label user-supplied odds with unknown availability or time and use them as conditional inputs. If the requested book's quote is unavailable, label any comparison price as another book's quote.

Look for a sportsbook's live board or [Covers](https://www.covers.com/sport/football/nfl/odds) and [VegasInsider](https://www.vegasinsider.com/nfl/odds/las-vegas/). ESPN's named sportsbook feed can be another source. Prefer a confirmed current snapshot and named-book comparisons. Confirm a displayed market is the active main line. Keep opening and retrospective closing observations dated and distinct from open-for-betting prices. Label meaningful differences across books. A line without juice is incomplete. If the price remains missing, stale, suspended, or otherwise unverified, mark that market **Pass** or **pending** and compare the other markets.

- **Spread.** Examine likely margin, game script, matchup, and price together. Crossing onto or off 3 or 7 changes win, push, and loss outcomes. Compare juice at each number and revisit the angle after a key move.
- **Total.** Examine projected opportunities and scoring efficiency, explosive plays, finishing, personnel, pace, roof and kickoff weather. Explain why the matchup could move scoring relative to the quoted total. Use opponent-adjusted evidence where available.
- **Moneyline.** Compare outright-win evidence with the price. A likely winner can be too expensive. Check the book's settlement rules for a tie when material.

Count correlated evidence once. Ask what the market already appears to price in and what could still surprise it. Give the best counterargument and a concrete reason to reassess, such as a new QB designation, roof decision or materially worse price. If the scenarios point in different directions, state both briefly and hold the affected market until the news and quote refresh. Continue evaluating independently supported markets. If only Leans qualify, present them as a watchlist and pass on a recommendation.

| Tier | Evidence at the quoted price |
| --- | --- |
| **Pass** | No supported value case, incomplete or stale quote, conflicting evidence, or unresolved information that could reverse the angle. |
| **Lean** | A directional case at a known quote, with limited support or sensitivity to a substantial counterargument. Watchlist only. |
| **Moderate** | Verified quote and core facts, multiple distinct supporting factors, a credible case for the price, and no unresolved issue likely to reverse it. |
| **Strong** | Meets Moderate criteria with unusually coherent evidence, corroborated critical facts, and limited material uncertainty. |

Use the tiers qualitatively. Reserve numerical win or cover estimates, exact fair lines, or expected value for a validated model. When reporting market-implied probability, identify it as a price conversion and distinguish it from an analyst forecast. Use a price limit only if the evidence supports it. Keep the scope to straight full-game markets.
